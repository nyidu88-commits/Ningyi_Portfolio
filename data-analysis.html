Climate & Ecology
Does China's Three-Step Terrain Still Matter? Climatic and Ecological Differentiation in a Warming Climate
Academic Project · Fall 2025
  • Used DEM, temperature, precipitation, and NDVI data across China's Three-Step Terrain to test whether the traditional framework still reflects present-day climatic and ecological patterns.
  • Applied ANOVA and Tukey's HSD to confirm significant differences across terrain steps, then used K-means and Gaussian Mixture clustering to identify seven finer eco-climatic regions.
  • Forecasted cluster area changes from 2020 to 2028, finding that humid mid-altitude and semi-arid high-plateau regions are expanding as the climate warms.

Air Quality
Air Quality Analysis and PM2.5 Forecasting in Guangzhou, China
Academic Project · Winter 2025
  • Cleaned and validated Guangzhou's 2020-2025 PM2.5 data from AirNow and integrated it with temperature and humidity records from the Guangzhou Statistical Yearbook.
  • Found strong seasonal and diurnal patterns: PM2.5 peaks in winter, driven by temperature inversions, low wind speeds, and heating and traffic emissions, and in the evening (8-11 PM); summer monsoon rainfall and afternoon thermal mixing drive concentrations down.
  • Built a Prophet time-series model forecasting PM2.5 through 2030, projecting a gradual rise starting around 2026 that suggests current policies may need to be strengthened with clean-energy and urban-planning measures.

Putah Creek Stream Discharge and Water Quality Study
Field Study, Putah Creek, CA · Spring 2024
  • Hand-measured stream discharge, temperature, and conductivity across the lower, middle, and upper reaches of Putah Creek, wading into the creek to collect velocity and depth readings directly in the field.
  • Found discharge dropped from about 10 m³/s just below Monticello Dam to about 3 m³/s midstream due to infiltration, evaporation, and agricultural and urban withdrawal, then rebounded to about 5 m³/s downstream from groundwater and tributary inputs.
  • Analyzed a 2020-2024 discharge time series, showing strong winter and spring peaks (up to about 35 m³/s) versus summer and early-fall lows (under 5 m³/s), with total annual discharge declining every water year from 2020-2021 through 2023-2024.
